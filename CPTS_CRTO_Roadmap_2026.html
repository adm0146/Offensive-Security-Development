Section 02 — The Distinction That Wins Both

Two exams, opposite temperaments

Most candidates train skills. The skills overlap almost completely between these two. What separates a pass from a fail is knowing which mode the exam grades — and not bringing the wrong one.

CPTS — The Thorough Pentester
Graded on coverage + the report

Find every path. Enumerate exhaustively. Tool-spray is fine. You want maximum coverage because flags are scattered and the report has to account for everything you touched.

The failure mode is missing a path or writing a weak report. Passers routinely fully own the box and still sweat the report — because the report is what they actually grade.

CRTO — The Quiet Operator
Graded 50% objective + 50% OPSEC

Reach the objective and don't get seen. Half your score is staying under Defender. Minimal footprint, deliberate tooling, every loud action justified or avoided.

The failure mode is getting detected. You can complete every objective and still fail at 85% if the OPSEC half tanks. Loud habits from CPTS will sink you here.

Carry this

Run CRTO like CPTS — exhaustive, noisy, spray-and-pray — and you fail on detection. Run CPTS like CRTO — surgical, minimal — and you leave flags and points on the table. Train the switch, not just the skills. Every rep from here forward, name the mode before you start.

Phase I — 10 Jun → 11 Jul · ~4.5 weeks

CPTS final prep

The hard prerequisite is already cleared and three ladder boxes are banked. Now close the AD gaps your completed boxes didn't cover, build an exam-proof field manual, and bank blind reps so the exam chain runs cold. The original Week 1–3 has been rebalanced to spend your surplus on the gaps and on blind boxes. AD carries the schedule because it's the slowest skill to build judgment for — the gaps below are iterative and only click with reps; the web/service foothold is bounded by comparison, so it earns a couple of targeted reps, not the calendar. Mode for the whole phase: thorough pentester.

What passers actually say

1. The path must be 100% complete to unlock the exam — done. ✓ The evaluation effectively started the moment you began the path, and you've cleared it. That's the one gate most candidates underestimate; it's behind you.

2. Enumeration is the whole game. The exam isn't "hard," it's about finding the right things. Manual enumeration with PowerView / ldapsearch / fuzzing beats hoping a tool flags it.

3. The report is what they grade. Read the Documentation & Reporting module to the letter and follow the template exactly — they don't forgive sloppy reports. Build the field manual now so you never Google mid-exam.

The two-pass rule · Weeks 1–2

New techniques get a guided first pass — module open, chain followed, gotchas understood. A first pass you don't understand isn't learning, it's flailing, and references exist to prevent exactly that.

The rule is the second pass: the moment a box is done, re-run its key chain blind, nothing open. Can't reproduce it cold? You watched it — go again. The blind redo is the proof the technique transferred, and it's why these weeks build independence from day one instead of dependence on the guide. Each acquisition week below ends with an exit check — those are the redos.

Week 1 · 10–15 Jun
ADCS gap-killers (pulled forward)

Three ADCS boxes covering the ESC variants that actually appear — ESC1 (the common case), ESC9 (the modern one), plus shadow credentials and pass-the-certificate. Run certipy find -vulnerable on each and reconcile against BloodHound; BloodHound misses edges, so confirm in the ADUC security tab natively.

  • CertifiedMediumassumed-breach ADCS + DACL abuse
    Learn ACL chaining (WriteOwner → GenericWrite → GenericAll); Shadow Credentials (msDS-KeyCredentialLink) via pywhisker / certipy shadow; pass-the-certificate over PKINIT; ESC9 (NoSecurityExtension + UPN swap to Administrator); mapping the full path in BloodHound CE.
  • EscapeMediumMSSQL → the canonical ESC1
    Learn MSSQL enumeration and xp_dirtree to coerce a Net-NTLMv2 hash (Responder); offline cracking; credential hunting in logs for lateral movement; ESC1 (Enrollee-Supplies-Subject → request a cert as any user); pass-the-hash.
  • AuthorityMediumESC1 with a twist + PassTheCert
    Learn cred hunting in Ansible IaC (ansible-vault → ansible2john → crack); Responder LDAP MITM against a misconfigured PWM; ESC1 enrollable by Domain Computers; abusing MachineAccountQuota (addcomputer.py) to get an enrollable principal; PassTheCert — authenticate over LDAPS/SChannel with the cert, no PtH needed.

Alongside the boxes, finish the ADCS Attacks module — that's where ESC1–ESC11 live as theory.

Exit checkBefore Week 2, re-run blind with nothing open: a full ESC1 request-and-authenticate (Escape / Authority) and the Shadow Credentials → PKINIT → pass-the-cert chain (Certified). If either needs the guide, it's not yours yet.

Week 2 · 16–22 Jun
RBCD, Kerberos-only + NTDS

The two hard boxes that force the techniques community checklists skip. Vintage is the centerpiece — the single box closest to the AD gaps you listed.

  • VintageHardKerberos-only AD, RBCD, gMSA, DPAPI
    Learn operating with NTLM disabled — Kerberos auth for every tool, even enumeration (the real mindset shift); pre-created / disabled computer-account takeover; gMSA read (ReadGMSAPassword); DPAPI offline extraction (dpapi.py); RBCD end-to-end (add an SPN with bloodyAD or borrow a SPN-holding account → AllowedToAct → S4U impersonation of DC$); DCSync to finish.
  • BlackfieldHardforced reset, LSASS, SeBackupPrivilege → NTDS
    Learn anonymous SMB/RID enumeration → user list; AS-REP roast + password spray; forced password reset over RPC (targeted ACL abuse); harvesting an LSASS dump and extracting creds with pypykatz; SeBackupPrivilege → DiskShadow / Copy-FileSeBackupPrivilege to copy NTDS.dit → secretsdump → DA; pass-the-hash.

No clean retired box isolates LDAP signing / channel binding or NTLMv1 downgrade — drill those in a relay lab: coerce auth, relay to LDAP with ntlmrelayx, and watch exactly when signing / channel binding blocks you. For DPAPI browser creds / credential manager, the Academy DPAPI section plus Vintage's offline DPAPI cover the mechanics; the browser/credmgr angle is mostly real-engagement territory — bake the loot commands into the field manual.

Exit checkBefore Week 3, re-run blind with nothing open: the full RBCD → S4U impersonation chain on Vintage, and the LSASS → SeBackupPrivilege → NTDS.dit → DCSync path on Blackfield. These are the two that close your listed gaps — they have to be cold before you move to blind boxes.

Week 4 · 30 Jun – 6 Jul

Run it blind: no references, enumerate → exploit → escalate → pivot → document, end to end. Clear Zephyr cold and produce a clean report from your notes and you're exam-ready.

Phase II — 12 Jul → ~22 Jul · 10-day window

CPTS exam execution

The window covers hacking and the report. Treat the report as a first-class deliverable from hour one, because it is the thing that gets graded.

Day 1
Read the engagement letter like a client doc

Scope, objectives, rules. From there it's fully blackbox — no handed CVEs, no obvious path. Set up the flag tracker and the evidence-capture habit before you touch a target. Screenshot everything you'd need to reconstruct a finding.

Days 2–6
Enumerate, chain, document in parallel
  • Methodical enumeration over clever exploitation. The flags reward finding the right thing, not the rarest exploit.
  • Document as you go. Every finding written up the moment you land it — not "later." Later is how the report eats your last three days and ships with mistakes.
  • Track flags against the 14. Pass is 12; you're going for 13–14, so chase the hard ones — but never at the cost of the report.
Days 7–9
Report — to the template, to the letter

Commercial-grade report: per-finding description, evidence, impact, and remediation, exactly as the module specifies. Watch the things passers get burned on — redactions, typos, your name rendering correctly, screenshots present. Reports run long (150–200 pages is normal); budget for it. Output PDF or ZIP, no password, under 20 MB.

GATE — report complete + format-checked before submit
Day 10
Submit, then stop

Submit. Results take up to 20 business days — do not wait for them to start CRTO. Note the safety net: even on a fail you must submit a report to be eligible for a free retake, and a second attempt can start immediately using the feedback. Submitting a real report is non-negotiable either way.

Interval — 23 Jul → 26 Jul · 3–4 days

Deliberate decompression

This is in the plan because burnout is the single most-reported way CPTS candidates stall out afterward. Three to four days fully off the keyboard. Golf, barbell, church, the people in your life. Then buy CRTO and come back sharp. Skipping this to "save time" costs more time than it saves.

Phase III — 27 Jul → 13 Sep · ~7 weeks

CRTO prep

Same kill chain, new discipline. Mode for the whole phase: quiet operator. The 2026 exam scores 50% objective + 50% OPSEC, so the entire phase is about reaching domain dominance and leaving almost no trace doing it.

Buy first · then the standard

Red Team Ops from Zero-Point Security — ~£399 (≈$540, PPP pricing varies by region), course + exam bundled, you can't buy the exam separately. Lifetime access, Cobalt Strike included, unlimited free retakes with a 7-day cooldown. ~20 hours of course material.

The standard for every chapter: reproduce the attack with Windows Defender ON, and write a separate note per lab. Passers are unanimous — the course alone is sufficient, but only if you can reproduce everything cold. Getting it to work with AV off teaches nothing the exam rewards.

Run the lab chains blind and OPSEC-clean: foothold → objective with Defender on and detections near zero, notes finalized per lab. Mind the small things that fail people — persistence so your beacon survives, and checking commands before you run them (wrong computer name pasted from a lab is a classic). When you can drop the objective file cleanly with no reference, launch the exam.

Reference B — Operating Cadence

How the hours get spent

The unit of work is a full-cycle rep

Enumerate → exploit → escalate → pivot → document. A half-rep that stops before documentation doesn't count. Target 30–40 quality reps before the CPTS exam.

Readiness has one definition

You can run the chain blind, with no reference, and produce clean documentation from your own notes. Anything short of that is "still studying," not "ready."

The field manual is the asset

Your notes are what keep you off Google during the exam and out of trouble on the job. Build it once, maintain it forever — it outlives both certs and follows you into the operator seat.

The pipeline already scales

Mac for analysis, opsbox for attacking, snapshots for safety. CRTO's Cobalt Strike teamserver is the same architecture one layer up. You're not learning a new workflow — you're adding C2 to the one you built.
